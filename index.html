01Overview

At the core, we're youth advocates and storytellers.

Why this project

Walker Post and Dillon St. Bernard have championed young people for their entire careers. Walker got his start as a journalist and digital marketer for nonprofits. He later founded Covalent to close the internship gap for college students, led youth-focused strategy for clients at DoSomething.org, and today coaches young leaders every week through Daybreakers and the Carnegie Young Leaders program. Much of his work at Daybreak revolves around working with clients around youth development and leadership.

Dillon co-founded Youth250 and built Team DSB, a Gen Z-led firm that has worked with the largest network and coalitions of young people, placed young people on platforms such as CNN and NBC News, and founded Webby Award-winning projects such as "Young People Address the Nation," the first-of-its-kind project that provided a national platform to young people. Daybreak and Team DSB are how we keep doing that work, and Summer Search's question, what leadership means and who gets to define it, is one we both work on every day.

The opportunity

Summer Search has always developed leaders. Its model of mentoring, transformative experiences and long-term support builds a capacity young people already have. The FY27–29 Strategic Plan makes that explicit, and the organization's language now needs to catch up. This work sharpens who Summer Search has been: it names leadership clearly enough that donors, partners, students and alumni recognize it, while keeping the educational, career and economic outcomes stakeholders already trust in view.

The risk runs in two directions. "Leadership" is a crowded and often generic word in the youth sector, so the definition has to be specific to Summer Search. And a new label without a clear definition can read as a departure, which the 35-year story should prevent.

Why now

A new strategic plan, new ways to reach more young people, and an effort to share the mentoring approach with adults and organizations beyond Summer Search all depend on one shared answer: what do we mean by leadership? Settling that answer before the plan reaches external audiences lets every new program, partnership and appeal build on the same language.

Young people are asking a version of the same question. A 2026 Gallup study with the Walton Family Foundation and Harvard's Making Caring Common project found that 45% of Gen Z report lacking meaning, purpose or both, and that young people who believe they make a positive impact on others are three to four times more likely to feel a sense of meaning and purpose. Leadership, as Summer Search develops it, gives young people that sense of impact.

What success looks like

By the end of March 2027, Summer Search will have a positioning and a definition of leadership its leaders agree on, a messaging framework stakeholders use in their own words, and its highest-priority communications already speaking the new language. Donors and partners will hear one consistent story about why leadership, why Summer Search and why now, tied to outcomes they already value. In addition, a select group of young Summer Search ambassadors will have shaped the work.

Questions we'll bring

  • Is "youth leadership organization" the strongest frame, and for which audiences?Some young people do not yet see themselves as leaders, and some funders fund outcomes before they fund leadership.

  • What does Summer Search mean by leadership that a peer organization could not also claim?

  • How directly should leadership connect to the college, career and economic outcomes donors already associate with Summer Search?

  • Who defines leadership: the organization, or the young people it serves?

02Our approach

Listen. Map. Build. Equip.

All Daybreak engagements move through four phases. Here is how they map to the four phases in your RFP.

Optional add-on

Youth Leadership Lab

Young people are the experts on what leadership looks like in their own lives, and the Youth Leadership Lab puts that expertise to work. Six to eight Summer Search students and alumni, drawn from all five regions, join two 90-minute participatory design sessions as co-designers of the positioning and the messaging.

Session one, during Map. Participants build their own definition of leadership from their experience in the program, then test "youth leadership organization" and the positioning options against it.

Session two, during Build. Participants work the draft messaging the way they would use it with peers, families and funders, rewrite the lines that miss, and bring the stories and proof points that make leadership real.

Summer Search gets a leadership definition co-authored by the people it describes, and messaging its students and alumni helped write.
